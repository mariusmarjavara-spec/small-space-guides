Fit a Home Office into a Bedroom Corner: 6 Fixes That Work

Most people who work from a bedroom are not short of ambition, only of floor. A corner can hold a perfectly good desk if you decide the walkway first, put the light where it helps, and stop the cables from taking over. These six fixes go in the order that avoids buying the wrong thing.

1. Decide the walkway before the desk

Measure from the corner to the nearest bed edge, wardrobe door or doorway, and keep at least 60 cm clear for walking. Whatever is left is the desk's footprint. People often buy a desk first and then discover the wardrobe door no longer opens.

Mark the footprint on the floor with painter's tape and live with it for a day. If you trip on it, it is too big.

2. Put the light to the side, not behind the screen

A window straight ahead causes glare on the screen, and a window directly behind causes reflections. Turn the desk so daylight arrives from the left or right, and keep the monitor roughly at right angles to the window.

If the corner is dark, a clip-on or small desk lamp at the side does more than a bright ceiling light, because it lights the keyboard without shining into your eyes.

3. Get the cables off the floor

In a small room the cables are the clutter that shows. Gather the plugs into one power strip and give that strip a home, then run the cords along the desk edge or underneath it instead of across the floor.

Two common ways to do this are a cover box for the power strip and a tray that sits under the desk. Which one fits depends on whether your desk has a free underside and how long your power strip is, so measure both before you buy anything.

4. Raise the screen to eye height

A laptop on a bare desk puts the screen too low and bends the neck forward. Raising it so the top of the screen sits near eye level, and using a separate keyboard, fixes the posture problem in one step.

Until you buy anything, a few sturdy books under the laptop does the same job. A foldable riser is the tidy version, and it packs flat when the desk has to be a desk again.

5. Keep the chair from blocking the room

A chair with arms and wheels eats the walkway you protected in step one. If the chair has to be pushed in to open the wardrobe, choose a smaller chair, or one without arms that slides fully under the desk.

A dining chair with a firm cushion is a reasonable stopgap, and it is easier to move.

6. Make the corner disappear at night

Working where you sleep is easier when work can be put away. Decide a shut-down habit: close the laptop, put it and the keyboard in a drawer or a box, and clear the surface so the corner looks like furniture again.

  • Measure the walkway and mark the desk footprint with tape
  • Turn the desk so daylight comes from the side
  • Gather plugs into one strip and give it a home
  • Raise the screen so its top is near eye level
  • Check the chair can be pushed fully under the desk
  • Clear the surface at the end of the day
